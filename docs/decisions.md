# Decisions log

Newest at the bottom. Each entry: date, decision, why, how to undo.

## 2026-10-04

* Model the car as it is in the photos, not the brief's spec sheet: barn doors, no flares, no side steps, no roof rails, 4500 EFI badge on the rear quarter. Why: photos are the visual target. Undo: add the parts and update docs/reference-notes.md.
* Procedural stand in shell built in Blender from the blueprint and photos, because no free LC100 shell was supplied and the usual download sites are blocked here. Why: unblocks the whole pipeline (naming, pivots, export, app) today. The Phase 2 paid model replaces it through the same contract with zero UI changes.
* One parts contract, src/data/parts.m1.json, read by Blender and the app. Group codes are marked verified, recalled (from memory, needs checking) or unknown (0000). Keys freeze once Saaqib verifies them against the EPC, before any DB seeding or public link.
* More meshes than the brief's 20 to 30 target (about 70): fenders, handles, mirrors, mud flaps, badges and wipers are separate because each is a sellable part. Draw calls stay far below any limit that matters.
* Blender runs as the bpy 4.5 LTS Python module from PyPI, since blender.org downloads are blocked.
* Draco mesh compression (not meshopt) for the GLB, because meshopt quantisation moves node transforms and would shift hinge pivots. Decoder is self hosted in public/draco.
* Garage lighting for M1 uses drei Lightformers (no HDRI download needed). Real HDRIs for the other scenes come in M3 once Poly Haven is reachable or files are supplied.

* Answers from Saaqib: trim is GXR (the G badge is normal on GCC GXR cars); battery sits on the side the engine bay photo shows (vehicle left), not the brief's right; chassis code unknown, assume FZJ100 with independent front suspension until confirmed.
* Assets supplied by Saaqib: five Poly Haven HDRIs in public/hdri (CC0, scene mapping in public/hdri/README.md), engine loops and ignition clips in public/audio (CC BY 4.0, attribution in public/audio/CREDITS.md, shown in the HUD credits).
* Garage scene uses autoshop_01: one Environment draws the ground projected backdrop, a second one lights and reflects with the same HDRI plus softbox Lightformers for paint highlights. Only the garage HDRI loads at start; the other scenes come in M3.
* Owner's manual text (US market, 2UZ-FE) is in reference/manual: use it for Toyota names and positions only, strip anything the GXR does not have. Official dimensions from it: length 4890, wheelbase 2850, tread 1620 front and 1615 rear.
* Headless screenshots use software WebGL, which is slow on a 700k triangle scene; the screenshot script waits up to 5 minutes for a frame. Real fps must be measured on a GPU machine.

## 2026-10-05

* Switched the car from the procedural stand in to Saaqib's downloaded LC100 2006 model (CC BY 4.0, commercial use allowed, attribution required and shown in the site credits). Why: the stand in read as a 90s car; the real geometry is the only route to photoreal. The procedural code stays as a fallback and as the source of our wheels, badges and wipers.
* Conversion is code, not hand editing: every source triangle is classified into a part key by region (gap lines measured from depth scans), so a future model swap is the same script with new numbers.
* GXR changes applied in the conversion: side steps and lower cladding deleted and replaced by clean lower door skins plus a dark rocker, roof spoiler and third brake light deleted, tailgate garnish recess patched and our LAND CRUISER plate placed, grille and mirrors black, chrome handles kept, leather textures desaturated to grey cloth, wood trims to grey plastic, the model's own GXR badge kept (it sits on the left back door; Saaqib's car has a G on the right, to confirm).
* Textured materials from the source are kept where they add detail (lamps, dash, cluster, seats) under tex_ names; everything else maps to the material library keys.
* M2 wheels: the model's 5 spoke VXR style wheel is replaced by a procedural six spoke 16x8 alloy (blender/parts/wheel6.py, SDF cast face plus the shared tire). The old 5 spoke stays in blender/parts/wheel.py; the build imports wheel6 as wheel in step_parts.
* M2 shading lives in the app, not in Blender: material names from the GLB are keys into TUNES in src/scene/car/look.ts, so finish changes never need a rebuild. Matched camera shots use the URL params cam, look and fov.
* Headlamp eyebrow and handle plate remnants are logged as open items; Saaqib said the defining can come later and to continue the flow.
* M3 backdrop: drei's Environment ground projection was never visible, because its dome (scale 1000) sat outside the camera far plane (100), so the flat HDRI background showed and the floor slid under the car when orbiting. Replaced by src/scene/SkyDome.tsx: same projection maths per true view ray, an 80 m dome inside the far plane, plus yaw and floor offset uniforms.
* M3 scene framing: each HDRI is turned (yaw) so the hero view works. The corniche skyline sits behind the car, the desert road and highway run along the car, and the dunes sun comes from the side. The highway HDRI was shot standing on a concrete median, so its floor is shifted 3.6 m and the car sits on the hard shoulder.
* M3 shadows: the brief asked for accumulative shadows for the parked state. drei AccumulativeShadows with three 0.186 renders its light map without any occlusion (checked: shadow maps exist, 149 casters, the plane receives, yet the map is uniform), and it would cost about 70 frames of extra passes on every scene switch. Instead the parked shadow is baked once in Blender Cycles (blender/bake_ground_shadow.py: shadow catcher, uniform sky plus overhead softbox) and drawn as a textured plane, free at runtime. The sun's hard shadow is live (directional light plus a shadow material floor), so it still follows the time of day. Doors opening do not change the baked part.
* M3 sun: each HDRI's sun direction is measured from its brightest pixels and stored in scenes.ts; the yaw turns it with the sky. Time of day lowers the sun, warms the light (a small warm grade in post) and dims the exposure. The sky itself is a photo and does not change colour; that is the limit of single HDRIs.
* M3 night: lamps glow per part, not per material, because front and rear lamps share material names. The tail lamp red is painted in the lamp texture under a clear lens, so a shader mask lights only the red areas (the reverse lamp stays dark). Brake, reverse, fog and turn lamps stay off: the car is parked. Night bloom uses a high threshold so it stays on the lamps, not on every city light.
* M3 loading: scene switches wait until the new HDRI has loaded (the old scene stays on screen meanwhile), and a sky starts preloading when its button is hovered or focused, instead of fetching all four 2k HDRIs (about 24 MB) up front.
* M3 open notes: goegap (the Liwa Dunes HDRI) is a rocky South African desert with sand, not a dune field; a real dunes HDRI can drop in later through scenes.ts. Heat haze (optional in the brief) is not done.
* M4 part card: shows only what the model and contract know (name, key, system, Toyota group with its confirmation status, parent part, fitment, open and close, copy link). OEM number, price, stock and WhatsApp ordering are stated as arriving with the M7 parts database instead of showing invented numbers.
* M4 hinges: every hinged part opens on its own (store keeps an open map); Open all and H still stagger them together. A click on a hinged part both opens its card and toggles it, as the brief asks.
* M4 X Ray: body, doors, glass, lamps and trim swap to a fresnel ghost material and stop casting shadows; wheels and interior stay solid. Ghosted meshes pass pointer events through, so the interior can be hovered and clicked. Hotspots for mechanical parts need the M7 hotspot positions; nothing mechanical exists as a mesh yet.
* M4 materials: night glow and X Ray are applied together from each mesh's base material (applyModes in src/scene/car/look.ts), so the modes combine and undo cleanly.
* M4 outline: postprocessing OutlineEffect needs the composer's autoClear off; without it the outline silently never draws.
* Contract: the interior parts lost their "(placeholder)" names, since they come from the real model now.
* M4 camera: Saaqib chose to do the camera choreography on Opus 5.5 instead of switching to Fable. Exterior moves interpolate on a sphere around the car so they swing round it instead of cutting through; cabin moves go through the open door (approach outside, dolly to the eye, small settle, door closes). In the cabin the orbit controls orbit a point 5 cm ahead of the eye, which turns them into look around; drag grabs the world.
* M4 floor: outside, the lowest allowed orbit angle is recomputed every frame from the target height and distance, so the camera never drops below 0.15 m, including under the raised car.
* M4 hotspots: 21 GXR controls from owner's manual pages 2 to 6 (US manual, stripped to the GXR: no nav, cruise, heaters, power tilt). Most positions were raycast on the model from the driver view; stalks, ignition, hazard switch and display are marked approximate.
* M4 lift: a simple procedural two post lift (posts, swing arms, pads) in the red of the lifts in the garage HDRI. Posts deploy, arms swing in, the car rises 1.55 m; the parked shadow fades as it rises. Garage only: leaving the garage lowers the car, Get In lowers it first.
* M4 engine bay: the source model's bay shows a V8 cover; the GXR's 1FZ-FE needs its own engine geometry (polish list).
* Parts source: toyotapartsdeal.com (shared by Saaqib) is blocked by the network policy, both from the container and the web fetch tool. It lists US market parts, so 1FZ-FE and GCC numbers still need checking against a GCC source.
* M5 engine model (src/engine/sim.ts): first order responses, not physics. Warm idle 650, cold fast idle 1150 settling over about 40 s, redline 5000, limiter at about 5080 with a short fuel cut that makes it bounce, full throttle from idle to redline in about 1.2 s (heavy inline six). Gearing for wheel speed on the lift: first 4.08 and final drive 4.10 are approximate H151F values, tire 275/70R16. Fuel shows 5/8, the odometer starts at 0 until Saaqib gives the real reading.
* M5 sound (src/engine/audio.ts): only the six cylinder engine-sim loops, crossfaded in pairs with equal power and pitched to the exact rpm; throttle opens a low pass filter. The starter crank is synthesized in Web Audio. The supplied ignition and running clips are not used: they are a different, already running engine and clash with the loops. Audio loads on the first Start Engine click (browsers need a gesture).
* M5 body motion: the body parts sit in a sprung group that rolls and pitches about a roll centre 0.55 m up, on spring dampers driven by the change in rpm (torque reaction), plus idle rock that is stronger right after a cold start. Wheels stay planted. On the lift the car is in first gear, so revving spins all four wheels (full time 4WD) and the speedo moves.
* M5 HUD: separate instrument cluster at the bottom (src/ui/Dash.tsx), needles on underdamped springs so they overshoot and settle. Space revs, E starts and stops, a hold button for touch.
